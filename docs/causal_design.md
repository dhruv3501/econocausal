Causal Design — EconoCausal

**1. Project Objective****
EconoCausal aims to estimate the causal effect of customer discounts on purchase behavior and revenue. The goal is to identify customers who are more likely to purchase because of a discount and support effective promotional budget allocation.

****2. Treatment****
The treatment variable is the discount offered to a customer.
- 0% discount — no discount
- 10% discount — low discount
- 20% discount — high discount
For the initial causal analysis, we also use a binary treatment variable:
- "discount_20 = 1" when the discount is 20%.
- "discount_20 = 0" when the discount is not 20%.

****3. Outcome Variables****
- Purchase: Whether the customer makes a purchase.
- Revenue: Revenue generated by the customer.
The initial synthetic dataset focuses on revenue as the outcome.

****4.Confounding Variables****
The following customer characteristics may influence both discount assignment and revenue:
- Age
- Income
- Tenure
- Past purchases
- Loyalty tier
- Days since last purchase
These variables are included in the initial synthetic dataset for causal analysis.

****5. Causal Assumptions****
1. Customer characteristics may influence the discount assigned.
2. Customer characteristics may influence revenue.
3. The discount may influence customer purchase behavior and revenue.
4. The analysis adjusts for observed confounders to estimate the causal effect.

****6. Causal Graph (DAG)****
The proposed causal relationships are:
- Customer characteristics → Discount assignment
- Customer characteristics → Revenue
- Discount assignment → Revenue

****7. Estimation Approach****
The initial analysis compares:
1. Naive revenue differences between discount groups.
2. DoWhy-based causal effect estimation.
3. Inverse Probability Weighting (IPW) using estimated propensity scores.
Further work will evaluate Double Machine Learning (DML) methods as specified in the project plan.

****8. Limitations****
The current dataset is synthetic. Initial estimates are exploratory and depend on the data-generation process and causal assumptions.
The binary 20% discount analysis compares the 20% group with the combined non-20% group, which includes both 0% and 10% discounts.
